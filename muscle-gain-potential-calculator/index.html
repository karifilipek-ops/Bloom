How this is calculated

Muscle potential: Casey Butt's model, built from the measurements of top drug-free bodybuilders. Bigger wrists and ankles mean a bigger frame, which can carry more muscle. Maximum lean mass (lb) = height1.5 × (√wrist ÷ 22.667 + √ankle ÷ 17.0104) × (1 + target body fat% ÷ 224), using inches.

Realistic vs. maximum: some "natural" champions in the original data likely weren't, and they were the genetic elite anyway. So treat the maximum as an absolute ceiling and the realistic number (95%) as what most dedicated lifters can expect.

Timeline: Lyle McDonald's estimates for gains per year of proper training: men about 20–25 lb in year 1, 10–12 lb in year 2, 5–6 lb in year 3, then 2–3 lb a year. Women gain about half that.

Women: Butt's model was built from male data. It's the best frame-based estimate available, but treat the results as a rough ceiling.
